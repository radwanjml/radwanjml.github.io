Meet Herrand

A delivery robot designed by simulation.

Six wheels, a self-levelling cargo pod and a 360° lidar, on a frame grown by an optimiser.

01 · The pod

Your order rides level.

The 40 L pod hangs on a pendulum yoke, so it self-levels over kerbs and slopes, swinging up to ±20° without touching the frame. The seam light says what it’s doing.

Grown, not drawn.

03 · Running gear

A 120 mm kerb, in the rain.

Six hub-motor wheels on a rocker-bogie keep at least four tyres on the ground as it climbs. On a wet kerb it needs grip of 0.39, and wet concrete gives 0.55, so there is margin to spare. It turns on the spot.

The making of

How it was designed.

Spec first, then maths, then a frame grown by an optimiser, then the checks. Each step is a script you can run again.

  1. 01

    Write the brief.

    EU pavements, 20 kg and about 40 L of cargo, a 120 mm kerb in the rain, under €1,700 in parts. Every later choice is scored against this list.

    Payload 20 kgKerb 120 mm, wetRange ≥ 20 kmParts ≤ €1,700Strength SF ≥ 2Clearance ≥ 15 mm
  2. 02

    Solve the layout before the looks.

    A wheel can’t climb a step as tall as its own radius. Too big, and the tyres overlap; the first layout did, by 64 mm. With a 20 mm gap between tyres, that leaves 121 to 130 mm. Herrand sits at 130.

  3. 03

    Pick the drivetrain on evidence.

    Six-wheel rocker-bogie against four tri-star clusters. The tri-star needs less grip, but jolts the cargo three times harder and has nearly three times the moving parts.

  4. 04

    Let physics draw the frame.

    A 3D topology optimiser got the load points and keep-outs for the wheels, then removed every gram that carried no load. After fifty iterations, a hoop around the pod and a truss to the axles were left.

    Side view at iterations 1 · 6 · 15 · 30 · 50

  5. 05

    Turn voxels into a casting.

    Rebuilt as tapered members, a strut lattice and bone-like joints, castable in A356 aluminium. The first sizing came out at 43 kg; the second, at 3.99 kg.

  6. 06

    Check everything.

    Linear static FE on the real geometry, a kinematic kerb climb, collision sweeps of every moving part, and a lidar raycast against the robot’s own body.

    18.4SF on yield
    28.3SF fatigue
    12.8SF, 30 cm drop
    40 mmmin clearance on the kerb
    0collisions, full travel
    2.2%lidar self-occlusion
  7. 07

    Keep the mistakes in.

    Seven were caught by these checks, from tyres through the body to a safety factor of 78 that a hand calculation said was 8. They’re listed below, because they’re the proof the checks work.

What went wrong

Seven mistakes, and what caught them.

Every one was found by a test, and every test still runs.

  1. Tyres through the bodyCaught by BVH collision tests that now run on every change.
  2. Wheels overlapping by 64 mmThe layout script never checked tyre clearance. Adding it gave layout v2.
  3. A frame through the wheelsThe optimiser didn’t know the wheels existed. Keep-out zones fixed it.
  4. A 43 kg frameA volume budget isn’t a mass target. The fix was two stages: topology first, then sizing.
  5. A safety factor of 78A shortcut said 78; a hand check said about 8. FE on the real geometry replaced the shortcut.
  6. Floating voxelsLoose islands gave nonsense deflections. Island removal fixed it.
  7. Seven geometry clashesThe bumper, side cameras, lid hinge, a bogie, the pod (twice) and the rear beam. The mechanics sims found them all, and all are fixed.

Honest limits

The honest limits.

It’s a concept study.The strength checks are linear static FE on 4 mm voxels. There has been no nonlinear, contact, casting-defect or physical testing, so this is not a validated design.
The hollow frame hasn’t been simulated yet.A wider hollow member has lower bending stress at equal mass, but thin-wall FE hasn’t been run yet.
Two clearances under my own rule.The rule is 15 mm. The pod sits 12.5 mm from the frame: accepted, because it swings its full ±20° without contact and closing the gap would cost cargo. A bogie sits at 14.8 mm: 0.2 mm short, flagged for the next revision.
Built with AI, directed by me.I worked with Claude as a collaborator: it wrote much of the code with me, and I made and can explain every design decision. My hand-coding is beginner level; my strengths are the 3D, the engineering judgement and the story.
Herrand is fictional.It is a brand made up for this portfolio piece. The film’s backgrounds are CC0 photographs from Poly Haven; the sound is CC0 and public-domain recordings from Wikimedia Commons plus my own synthesis.
