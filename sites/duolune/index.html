Duolune

Lounge chair N°1 · a concept by Radwan Jameel

Two half-moons, and seventeen tailored rollers hanging between them. Twelve ways to finish it, and any colour you like.

01/ 12 Cognac leather, satin black

Duolune began as a sketch of two arcs with a curve hanging between them. Turning it into a chair you could build meant working out how the seat holds on to the frame. The answer is six bolts, and you can see every one.

The pattern book

Four pieces make the chair

Piece 01

The arc

Each side is one half-circle, 1,100 mm across. That one line is the leg and the armrest at once. The seat hangs inside it like a hammock in a doorway.

Steel: 110 × 55 mm hollow section, 3 mm wall, about 13 kg a side. Wood: plies 1.8 mm thick, bent over a form.

Piece 02

The hang

Three rollers on each side carry a through-axle. A steel spacer crosses the gap to the rail, and an acorn nut closes it. Six bolts hold up the whole seat, and none of them is hidden.

Piece 03

The lens

From above, the rollers swell from 450 mm at the head and foot to 825 mm at the hips. Your body is wider in the middle, so the seat is too.

Piece 04

The seam

Each roller is a tailored tube: a flat end panel, welt piping round the edge, a covered button, and one row of top-stitching 16 mm in. Underneath runs a double-stitched seam you'll only see if you look for it.

        How long does it take?

        Six to eight weeks from order. Each chair is cut and sewn to your configuration, so nothing waits in a warehouse.

        What does delivery include?

        Two people bring it in, bolt the seat to the arcs and take the packaging away. The seat comes off the frame in one piece, so it fits through a standard 800 mm door.

        How do I look after leather and fabric?

        Leather: wipe with a damp cloth, condition twice a year, keep it out of direct sun. Wool, bouclé and velvet: vacuum with a soft brush; blot spills, don't rub them.

        Can a single roller be replaced?

        Yes. Every roller is fixed to the spine on its own, so one damaged roller is swapped without touching the others.

        Is the seat really that low?

        The lowest roller sits 290 mm off the floor. It's a lounger: you sink in with your feet forward. If you need to get up easily, ask about the raised frame.

        Can I order one?

        Not yet. Duolune is a concept, and the prices on this page are illustrative. If you make furniture and want to build it, get in touch below.

        How this was made

        One model, start to finish

        1. Model

          The shape was modelled in Blender. Its first version had no way for the seat to hold on to the frame, so the joints were engineered afterwards: real axles, real spacers, real sizes.

        2. Material

          Every finish on this page is procedural: grain, stitching, the seams and the ply lines in the wood. Nothing on this page is a photograph.

        3. Film

          The film at the top is one slow turn around the chair. Every two seconds the finish changes, through all twelve signature builds, and the camera never cuts.

        4. Web

          The chair you turned above is about a megabyte (half that on phones). Its shading, the soft shadows where parts meet and the light bouncing off the floor, was baked in Cycles; the stitching and the stripe are drawn by the graphics card as you look, so changing a colour costs nothing to download.
