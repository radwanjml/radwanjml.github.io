Why a lift at all

The research, graded.

Nobody has measured "3D websites" as one thing. What has been measured is the parts: 3D and AR product views, video, and speed. Grade A is independent or a controlled test. B is a platform's own data. C is one brand's case study.

  • Grade A · peer-reviewedAR lifted sales most for smaller brands.

    A 2022 Journal of Marketing study of a cosmetics retailer: the effect was small overall, and largest for less popular brands, pricier products and first-time buyers.

    Tan, Chandukala & Reddy, 2022 ↗
  • Grade A · A/B testFaster pages, 8% more sales.

    Vodafone tested two versions of a landing page. The one that loaded 31% faster sold 8% more. Heavy 3D can cost you this, so it has to be built light.

    web.dev, Vodafone ↗
  • Grade B · platform data94% higher conversion with 3D.

    Shopify's own average for merchants who added 3D content against photos alone. Self-reported, method not published.

    Shopify ↗
  • Grade B · 37 brands0.1 seconds, 8.4% more conversions.

    Google and Deloitte across 30 million sessions: a tenth of a second faster on mobile lifted retail conversion 8.4%.

    Milliseconds Make Millions ↗
  • Grade B · 7,196 campaignsVideo: 37% funded vs 15%.

    Kickstarter projects with a video were funded far more often. Correlation, not proof, but every dataset points the same way.

    Stephen Follows ↗
  • Grade C · one brand27% more likely to order.

    Rebecca Minkoff on Shopify: after shoppers interacted with a product in 3D they were 44% more likely to add it to cart.

    Shopify Plus case ↗

The honest part

An estimate, not a promise.

The lift is an assumption you choose. Good visuals help most when buyers can't touch the product: sold online, not launched yet, or hard to explain from a photo.

The fair way to know is to test it. Show half your visitors the new version for two weeks and compare. I'm happy to set that up.
